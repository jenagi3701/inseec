# Kizuna 絆 — pitch summary

**One-sentence positioning**
Kizuna helps people in Lyon make friends through Japanese pop culture and shared hobbies: small-group activities that turn into recurring circles, so you meet through what you love and keep seeing the same people.

*FR : « Rencontre-toi par ce que tu aimes. Fais des activités ensemble. Revois-toi naturellement. »*

---

### The problem
Making friends as an adult, especially after moving to a new city, is hard. Social formats are one-off (a dinner with strangers, then back to zero), online-only (Discord, forums), or too big and intimidating for shy people. Friendship needs **repeated, low-pressure contact with the same people**, and nothing is built for that.

### The solution
1. **Interest graph profile**: anime and manga genres, franchises, board games, drawing, Japanese language, cooking… Beginners are welcome.
2. **Activities matched to passions *and* social energy**: calm, creative, competitive, social or cultural. Groups are small, icebreakers are provided, and every activity is in a public place.
3. **Continuity loop**: after each activity, a private *"Envie de revoir ce groupe ?"*. When the wish is shared, the group becomes a **circle** with its next meeting proposed. The home screen separates *Retrouver* (circles, familiar faces) from *Découvrir* (new activities).

### Competitive differentiation
| | Timeleft | Meetup / event apps | Discord / forums | **Kizuna** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry point | Personality quiz | Event topic | Fandom | **Passions + social energy** |
| Format | Dinner with strangers | Mostly large events | Online | **Small-group activity** |
| After the event | New strangers next time | No follow-up | Stays online | **Same circle, mutual connections** |
| Shy-friendly | Medium | Low | High (online) | **High, in real life** |

Distinctive features: **Same Circle**, **Interest Graph**, **Familiar Faces**, **Discreet mutual connection** (no rejection, no friend counts) and **Social-energy filter**.

### MVP (working prototype)
Landing page, sign-up and 6-step onboarding, a dashboard with explained recommendations, discovery with filters, activity pages (practical info, participants, icebreakers, group thread), join/leave with live counts, saved activities, agenda, history and feedback, the "meet again" follow-up and circle creation, a My Circles page and circle pages, proposing an activity, member profiles, notifications, privacy settings, report and block, guidelines, and data export and deletion. It is responsive and French-first.
*Demo data only. Authentication, chat, payments and other members' answers are simulated.*

### Business model hypothesis
- The social core (discovery, circles, connections) is free.
- **Partner venues** (board-game cafés, manga shops, karaoke, Japanese restaurants) pay for filled off-peak group nights.
- **Commission** of 10–15% on ticketed workshops (ramen, calligraphy, escape rooms).
- Later: **organizer Pro tools** and an optional **membership**, only once repeat demand is proven.

### KPIs
North star: **% of users attending a 2nd activity with the same circle within 30 days.**
Also tracked:
- Onboarding completion
- View-to-join conversion
- Show-up rate
- "Meet again" rate
- Active recurring circles
- D30 retention
- Satisfaction ≥4/5
- Referral rate

### Next steps to validate
1. **Concierge pilot (4 weeks, Lyon)**: run 8–10 small activities by hand with 2–3 partner venues and university Japanese clubs. Use the prototype as the front end and handle group follow-ups manually.
2. **Measure the continuity thesis**: "meet again = yes" rate and actual second attendance (targets: ≥40% / ≥30%).
3. **Organizer interviews** (10) and **venue interviews** (5): willingness to run recurring nights, and to pay or share revenue.
4. **User interviews** (15) with newcomers and shy fans: price sensitivity and safety expectations.
5. **Decide**: if repeat attendance holds, build the backend, real chat and the organizer console. If not, rethink the format before scaling.
